# Installation Guide — Mikano Power Equipment

## Overview

Proper installation is critical for equipment safety, performance, and warranty validity. All Mikano generators, inverters, and electrical panels must be installed by certified Mikano technicians or authorized installation partners.

---

## Pre-Installation Requirements

### Site Survey

Before any installation, a Mikano-certified engineer must conduct a site survey to assess:

- **Load calculation** — Total connected load in kVA/kW. The generator/inverter must be sized at minimum 20% above the total connected load to account for motor starting currents and future expansion.
- **Physical space** — Minimum clearances: 1.5m on all sides of a generator for ventilation and maintenance access. Indoor installations require additional space for exhaust ducting.
- **Foundation** — Generators above 30kVA require a concrete foundation. Foundation dimensions and depth depend on the generator weight and local soil conditions. A vibration isolation plan must be included.
- **Fuel storage** — Location of the fuel tank relative to the generator. Maximum suction head for the fuel pump. Compliance with local fire safety regulations.
- **Exhaust routing** — Exhaust pipe routing must comply with local emission and noise regulations. Residential installations in Lagos require exhaust pipes to discharge at a minimum height of 3 meters above ground level.
- **Electrical infrastructure** — Existing panel capacity, cable routing distances, earth/ground electrode locations, and automatic transfer switch (ATS) placement.
- **Noise assessment** — Predicted noise levels at property boundaries. Acoustic enclosures may be required for noise-sensitive locations.

### Customer Approvals Required

Before installation begins, the customer must provide:

1. Signed installation agreement with equipment specifications
2. Site access confirmation and scheduling
3. Civil foundation readiness (if applicable)
4. Electrical permits from the local utility/regulatory body
5. Confirmation of existing electrical infrastructure condition

## Generator Installation Procedure

### Step 1: Foundation and Positioning

- Verify that the foundation meets the specifications from the site survey.
- Position the generator on the foundation using anti-vibration mounts.
- Level the generator using a spirit level — maximum allowable deviation is 1 degree in any direction.
- Secure the generator to the foundation using anchor bolts torqued to specification.

### Step 2: Fuel System Connection

- Connect the fuel supply line from the day tank or main fuel tank to the generator fuel inlet.
- Install a fuel return line from the generator back to the tank.
- Bleed all air from the fuel system before first start.
- Verify that fuel shutoff valves are accessible and properly labeled.
- Install a fuel filter/water separator if not factory-fitted.

### Step 3: Exhaust System Installation

- Connect the exhaust pipe to the engine exhaust manifold using a flexible coupling to absorb vibration.
- Route the exhaust pipe according to the installation plan. All horizontal runs must slope downward away from the engine to prevent condensation backflow.
- Install a rain cap or exhaust deflector at the termination point.
- Apply high-temperature sealant at all joints.
- Insulate or guard any exhaust sections within reach of personnel (surface temperature can exceed 500°C).

### Step 4: Cooling System Setup

- Verify that the coolant is pre-filled to the correct level.
- For remote radiator installations, connect coolant supply and return lines.
- Ensure adequate airflow for the radiator — minimum air inlet area must be 1.5x the radiator face area.
- Install a thermostatic fan controller if not factory-fitted.

### Step 5: Electrical Connections

- Install the Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS) at the main distribution board.
- Run power cables from the generator output terminals to the ATS.
- Cable sizing must comply with BS 7671 or NEC standards. Minimum conductor size is determined by the generator rated current plus a 25% safety margin.
- Connect the neutral-earth bond at the generator (TN-S system) or at the ATS (TN-C-S system) as specified in the installation plan.
- Install earth electrodes: one at the generator and one at the ATS. Earth resistance must be below 5 ohms.
- Connect control cables for remote start/stop and ATS control signals.

### Step 6: Battery Installation

- Install the starting battery on the battery tray.
- Connect battery cables: positive (+) first, then negative (−).
- Apply anti-corrosion spray to battery terminals.
- Verify battery voltage: minimum 12.4V for 12V systems, 24.8V for 24V systems.

## Inverter Installation Procedure

### Step 1: Location Selection

- Install the inverter in a cool, dry, well-ventilated location.
- Mount on a solid wall using the provided bracket. The wall must support at least 2x the inverter weight.
- Maintain minimum clearances: 30cm above, 30cm below, 15cm on each side.
- Avoid locations near heat sources, direct sunlight, or water.

### Step 2: Battery Bank Setup

- Place batteries on a battery rack or shelf. Never place directly on concrete floors (causes discharge).
- Connect batteries in series/parallel configuration as specified in the inverter manual.
- Use correctly sized battery cables — undersized cables cause voltage drop and overheating.
- Install a DC circuit breaker between the battery bank and the inverter.
- Verify total battery bank voltage matches the inverter input voltage (12V, 24V, or 48V).

### Step 3: AC Wiring

- Connect the mains input to the inverter AC input terminals through a dedicated circuit breaker.
- Connect the inverter AC output to the protected load panel.
- Do not connect loads exceeding the inverter's continuous power rating.
- Install a changeover mechanism if the inverter does not have built-in automatic transfer.

### Step 4: Earthing

- Connect the inverter earth terminal to the main earth bar.
- Ensure the earth electrode resistance is below 5 ohms.

## Commissioning Checklist

After installation is complete, the commissioning engineer must verify and sign off each item:

### Mechanical Checks

- [ ] Generator is level and securely anchored
- [ ] Anti-vibration mounts are correctly installed
- [ ] Fuel system is leak-free and properly bled
- [ ] Exhaust system is properly connected with no leaks
- [ ] Coolant is at the correct level and concentration
- [ ] All guards and covers are in place

### Electrical Checks

- [ ] Cable connections are torqued to specification
- [ ] Earth electrode resistance is below 5 ohms
- [ ] ATS operates correctly on mains fail and mains restore
- [ ] Generator output voltage is within ±5% of rated voltage
- [ ] Generator output frequency is 50Hz ±0.5Hz
- [ ] Phase rotation is correct (three-phase systems)
- [ ] All protection settings are correctly configured (overcurrent, earth fault, over/under voltage)

### Functional Tests

- [ ] Generator starts on simulated mains failure
- [ ] ATS transfers load within 10 seconds of mains failure
- [ ] ATS retransfers to mains within 60 seconds of mains restoration (with adjustable delay)
- [ ] Generator shuts down after retransfer and cooldown period
- [ ] Emergency stop button operates correctly
- [ ] Remote start/stop functions correctly (if applicable)
- [ ] Load test: 50% load for 30 minutes, then 100% load for 30 minutes
- [ ] All alarms and shutdowns operate correctly (oil pressure, high temperature, overspeed, overcrank)

### Documentation

- [ ] Serial numbers recorded (generator, engine, alternator, ATS, inverter)
- [ ] Installation photographs taken (minimum: front, back, left, right, electrical connections, earth electrodes)
- [ ] Commissioning report completed and signed by engineer and customer
- [ ] Warranty registration submitted to Mikano
- [ ] Customer training conducted (start/stop, emergency shutdown, weekly checks, who to call for service)

## Post-Installation Testing

The customer is entitled to a 72-hour monitoring period after commissioning. During this period:

1. The system operates under normal load conditions
2. The commissioning engineer remains on-call
3. Any deficiencies discovered are corrected at no additional cost
4. A final sign-off is obtained after the monitoring period

## Common Installation Errors to Avoid

| Error | Consequence | Prevention |
|-------|-------------|------------|
| Undersized cables | Overheating, voltage drop, fire risk | Always calculate cable size based on rated current + 25% |
| No anti-vibration mounts | Structural damage, excessive noise | Always use manufacturer-supplied mounts |
| Inadequate ventilation | Engine overheating, reduced performance | Verify airflow calculations during site survey |
| Incorrect earth bonding | Electric shock risk, protection malfunction | Follow the earthing plan and test electrode resistance |
| Missing fuel return line | Fuel system pressurization, injector damage | Always install both supply and return lines |
| Battery installed backwards | Control panel damage, fire risk | Verify polarity before connecting |
