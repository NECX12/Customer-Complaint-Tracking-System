# Safety Procedures — Mikano Power Equipment

## Overview

Safety is the top priority in all Mikano equipment operations. This document covers mandatory safety procedures for the operation, maintenance, and servicing of generators, inverters, and electrical panels. Non-compliance with these procedures may result in serious injury, death, or equipment damage.

---

## Fuel Handling Safety

### Storage Requirements

- Store diesel fuel in approved containers only (metal or approved plastic drums with proper seals).
- Fuel storage areas must be at least 3 meters away from any running generator or heat source.
- Maximum storage at a customer site without a fire safety certificate is 1,000 liters.
- All fuel storage areas must have a fire extinguisher (minimum 9kg dry powder or CO2) within 5 meters.
- Fuel drums must be properly labeled with fuel type and date of storage.

### Refueling Procedures

- **NEVER refuel a running generator.** Always shut down the engine and allow it to cool for at least 5 minutes before refueling.
- **No smoking** within 10 meters of fuel handling operations.
- Ground the fuel container to the generator frame before pouring to prevent static discharge.
- Use a funnel with a filter screen to prevent debris from entering the fuel tank.
- Wipe up any spilled fuel immediately. Fuel-soaked rags must be disposed of in a sealed metal container.
- After refueling, verify the fuel cap is properly sealed before restarting.

### Fuel Quality

- Use only clean, filtered diesel fuel (EN 590 or equivalent specification).
- Do not use contaminated, mixed, or aged fuel (fuel older than 6 months degrades and may damage injectors).
- Water in fuel causes injector damage and engine failure. Drain the water separator regularly.
- In tropical climates, treat stored fuel with a biocide every 3 months to prevent microbial growth.

## Electrical Safety

### General Electrical Safety Rules

1. **Only qualified, Mikano-certified electricians may work on electrical connections.** Unqualified personnel must never open electrical panels or modify wiring.
2. **Always de-energize before servicing.** Use the lockout/tagout (LOTO) procedure before performing any electrical work.
3. **Treat all conductors as live** until verified dead using a properly rated voltage tester.
4. **Never bypass safety devices** — circuit breakers, fuses, and protection relays exist to prevent fires and electrocution.
5. **Use insulated tools rated for the working voltage** when performing electrical work.
6. **Never work alone** on high-voltage equipment (above 400V). A safety observer must be present.

### Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) Procedure

1. Notify all affected personnel that the equipment will be de-energized.
2. Shut down the generator/inverter using the normal stop procedure.
3. Open the main circuit breaker and remove the key (if applicable).
4. Apply a lockout device to the circuit breaker.
5. Attach a danger tag with your name, date, and reason for lockout.
6. Verify that the equipment is de-energized by testing all phases with a voltage tester.
7. Earth/ground the conductors before touching them if the system voltage exceeds 400V.

### Arc Flash Safety

- Generators above 100kVA produce arc flash hazards at the output terminals.
- Wear appropriate PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) rated for the incident energy level.
- Minimum PPE for working near energized generator terminals: safety glasses, arc-rated face shield, insulated gloves (Class 0 minimum), arc-rated clothing.
- Maintain the minimum approach distance specified in the arc flash analysis.

### Earthing/Grounding Safety

- All generator frames, fuel tanks, and metallic enclosures must be connected to the earth electrode system.
- Earth electrode resistance must be tested annually. Maximum acceptable resistance is 5 ohms.
- A broken or disconnected earth conductor is a life-threatening hazard. Report immediately.

## Noise Compliance

### Regulatory Limits

Mikano generators must comply with the following noise limits at the property boundary:

| Location Type | Maximum Noise Level (dBA) at Boundary |
|--------------|---------------------------------------|
| Residential area — daytime (7am-10pm) | 65 dBA |
| Residential area — nighttime (10pm-7am) | 55 dBA |
| Commercial area | 75 dBA |
| Industrial area | 85 dBA |

### Noise Reduction Measures

- **Acoustic enclosures** — All generators above 30kVA should be fitted with sound-attenuated enclosures rated to achieve boundary compliance.
- **Exhaust silencers** — Residential and critical (hospital) installations require hospital-grade exhaust silencers rated for 25dBA+ attenuation.
- **Anti-vibration mounts** — Reduces structure-borne noise transmission.
- **Distance** — Position generators as far as possible from boundaries and occupied buildings.
- **Barriers** — Solid walls or purpose-built acoustic barriers can reduce noise by 10-15 dBA.

### Noise Measurement

- Noise measurements must be taken at the nearest property boundary.
- Use a calibrated sound level meter set to A-weighting (dBA) and slow response.
- Measurements should be taken at 1.5 meters above ground level.
- Record the ambient noise level (generator off) and the total noise level (generator running at rated load).

## Emergency Shutdown Procedures

### Generator Emergency Shutdown

In any of the following situations, perform an emergency shutdown immediately:

**Situations requiring emergency shutdown:**
- Fire in or near the generator
- Fuel leak near hot surfaces
- Unusual loud metallic noises from the engine
- Visible smoke from the engine compartment (not normal exhaust)
- Coolant spray or steam leak
- Person in contact with energized equipment
- Any immediate threat to life

**Emergency shutdown procedure:**
1. Press the **EMERGENCY STOP** button (red mushroom-head button on the control panel).
2. If the emergency stop button is inaccessible, switch off the fuel supply valve.
3. Move away from the generator to a safe distance (minimum 5 meters).
4. If there is a fire, use the nearest fire extinguisher ONLY if safe to do so. Use CO2 or dry powder — **NEVER use water** on an electrical fire or near fuel.
5. Call the site emergency number and Mikano emergency service line.
6. Do not attempt to restart the generator until a Mikano-certified technician has inspected it and cleared it for operation.

### Inverter Emergency Shutdown

**Situations requiring emergency shutdown:**
- Burning smell from the inverter or battery area
- Visible smoke or sparks
- Battery acid leak (sulfuric acid — corrosive)
- Swollen or deformed batteries
- Inverter display showing critical fault (E01–E05)
- Flooding or water ingress in the inverter room

**Emergency shutdown procedure:**
1. Switch off the inverter using the front panel OFF button.
2. Open the DC breaker between the battery bank and the inverter.
3. Open the AC input breaker.
4. If there is a battery acid spill, do not touch it. Acid burns require immediate medical attention — flush affected skin with clean water for 20 minutes.
5. Ventilate the room — batteries produce hydrogen gas which is explosive.
6. Call Mikano emergency service line.

## Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) Requirements

| Activity | Required PPE |
|----------|-------------|
| Refueling | Safety gloves, safety glasses |
| Generator routine checks | Steel-toe boots, safety glasses |
| Electrical work (below 400V) | Insulated gloves (Class 0), safety glasses, insulated tools |
| Electrical work (above 400V) | Insulated gloves (Class 1+), arc-rated face shield, arc-rated clothing, insulated tools |
| Battery maintenance | Chemical-resistant gloves, splash-proof goggles, apron |
| Exhaust system work | Heat-resistant gloves, face shield |
| Working at height (rooftop installations) | Safety harness, hard hat |

## Incident Reporting

All safety incidents, near-misses, and equipment damage must be reported to Mikano within 24 hours using the incident report form. Reports must include:

1. Date, time, and location of the incident
2. Equipment model and serial number
3. Description of what happened
4. Injuries sustained (if any)
5. Immediate actions taken
6. Photographs of the scene and damage
7. Names of witnesses

> **IMPORTANT**: Failure to report safety incidents may void the equipment warranty and expose the customer to regulatory penalties.
